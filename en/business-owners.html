Three routes, one outcome: your company operating legally in the US

E-1 / E-2

Treaty Trader and Investor

For nationals of countries with a treaty with the United States. The E-2 requires a substantial, real investment in a business that actively operates. Renewable indefinitely as long as the business stays active.

L-1

Intracompany Transfer

If you have a company operating outside the US, you can open or use a subsidiary to transfer executive, managerial or specialized staff, from your key team through to yourself. It's also the route for companies already in the US that need people from abroad.

EB-5

Immigrant Investor

A direct route to permanent residency based on capital investment that creates jobs in the United States. The right option when the Green Card is the goal from the start.

Frequently asked

What's the difference between the E-2 and the EB-5?

The E-2 is a treaty visa, non-immigrant but renewable indefinitely, for nationals of countries with a treaty with the US. The EB-5 is a direct route to permanent residency based on a larger investment and job creation.

How much capital do I need for an E-2 visa?

There's no fixed minimum in the law. It has to be substantial relative to the type of business, and enough to guarantee the business can operate. We assess it case by case.

Can I buy an existing business to qualify?

Yes. Buying a running business is one of the three routes we work with, alongside opening a new company or expanding an existing operation.

My US company needs to hire talent from abroad. Can you help?

Yes. We work with companies already established in the United States that need to bring key people in from abroad, designing the full transfer strategy through pathways like the L-1.
